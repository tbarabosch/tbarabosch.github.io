For a while, the homepage was one reverse-chronological list of articles. That worked when the list was short. Once older posts and several rather different technical subjects accumulated, finding anything became an exercise in remembering when I had written it. Scrolling is technically navigation, but only technically.

The most important change is that the blog now has a proper map. The [homepage](/) groups recent work into five curated areas, and the complete [topics index](/topics/) collects the relevant articles under systems security, reverse engineering, threat research, incident response, and AI engineering. Recent changes still appear first, so new and substantively updated posts remain visible without burying the older material.

There are two more direct routes into the archive. [Search](/search/) filters titles, excerpts, topics, and tags locally in the browser; the query does not leave the page. The complete [archive](/archive/) keeps the traditional chronological view for readers who know roughly when something appeared.

Individual posts received a few smaller conveniences as well. Code blocks can be copied without also copying a decorative label, section headings have permanent links, and local technical images open at full resolution. Post metadata now distinguishes publication and update dates and includes an estimated reading time. At the end of an article, the new links make it easier to report a correction through GitHub or email, subscribe to the full-content [RSS feed](/feed.xml), or connect with me on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/thomas-barabosch-3328b4256/).
